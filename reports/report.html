01

Dataset and Analytical Framework

The Ames Housing dataset records 2,930 residential property transactions across Ames, Iowa, covering 82 variables that describe the physical, spatial, and qualitative characteristics of each property alongside its final sale price.

The dataset is extensively documented through a structured metadata file that classifies each variable as continuous, discrete, ordinal, or nominal, and enumerates the valid levels for categorical fields. This metadata was parsed programmatically to support missingness analysis and to cross-validate the observed variable structure against documented expectations.

Analytical Objectives

Two modeling tasks were pursued concurrently throughout this analysis. The first is a regression task: predict the exact sale price of a property from its observed characteristics. The second is a binary classification task: determine whether a property belongs to the top price tier — defined as the upper third of the training sale price distribution — or not. Together, these tasks allow the analysis to address both continuous price prediction and categorical price-tier discrimination, using complementary methodological frameworks.

Evaluation Protocol

The dataset was partitioned into three disjoint subsets. The training set (70% of observations) was used to estimate all model parameters, preprocessing statistics, and grouping rules. The validation set (15%) was used exclusively for model comparison and hyperparameter selection. The test set (the remaining 15%) was withheld until all modeling decisions were finalised, and was used once to assess generalization performance. No information from the validation or test sets was permitted to influence any fitted quantity.

Notebook Structure

The project is organized across three notebooks with strict separation of responsibilities. 01_eda.ipynb covers exploratory analysis only and does not modify the data. 02_preprocessing.ipynb handles all data preparation and exports processed datasets to disk. 03_modeling.ipynb reads those processed files and contains all modeling logic, with no preprocessing code. This structure ensures that each notebook runs independently and that the modeling results are fully reproducible from the saved datasets.

02

Exploratory Analysis

The exploratory phase documented the dataset's structure, variable distributions, missing-value patterns, and inter-variable relationships before any data modification.

Variable Landscape

A profiling table was constructed for all 82 variables, reporting completeness, cardinality, data type, and summary statistics. Of the 82 variables, 43 are numeric (continuous and discrete) and 39 are categorical (nominal and ordinal). Numeric variables span a wide range of measurement scales, from counts (number of rooms, bathrooms) to areas measured in square feet and time-based variables (year built, year sold).

The target variable, SalePrice, exhibits strong right skew: the distribution has a long upper tail driven by a small number of premium properties. The interquartile range spans approximately $129,500 to $213,500, while individual sales extend well above $500,000. This skew motivated the use of log(SalePrice) as the regression target.

Overall quality (Overall Qual) has the highest Pearson correlation with sale price at r ≈ 0.80, followed by Gr Liv Area (above-ground living area) at r ≈ 0.71 and Garage Cars at r ≈ 0.64.

Missing Value Analysis

Twenty-seven variables contain missing values. These were systematically classified into two categories based on domain knowledge and metadata review.

Structural missingness covers variables for which NA is not a data error but a semantically meaningful state — the feature is absent rather than unrecorded. This category includes all basement variables (Bsmt Qual, Bsmt Cond, Bsmt Exposure, BsmtFin Type 1, BsmtFin Type 2), all garage variables (Garage Type, Garage Finish, Garage Qual, Garage Cond), masonry veneer variables, and several amenity indicators (Pool QC, Fence, Alley, Misc Feature, Fireplace Qu). Together these account for 25 of the 27 incomplete variables.

Non-structural missingness affects two variables: Lot Frontage (486 missing observations) and Electrical (1 missing observation). A targeted MCAR test for Lot Frontage — regressing a missingness indicator on theoretically motivated predictors including neighborhood, lot configuration, and building type — found significant associations, providing evidence of Missing At Random (MAR) rather than MCAR. This distinction is consequential for imputation strategy.

Correlation and Association Structure

The Pearson and Spearman correlation matrices were computed for all numeric pairs. Both metrics identify the same top predictors, suggesting that the observed correlations are not an artefact of linear assumptions. A Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) analysis of the top ten correlated predictors identified multicollinearity between area-related variables: Total Bsmt SF, Gr Liv Area, and 1st Flr SF are strongly intercorrelated, motivating the use of an aggregated total-area feature rather than multiple disaggregated area measures.

Cramér's V was computed for categorical variable pairs with cardinality of 15 or below. The highest associations were found among quality and condition ratings, which are rated on similar scales by the same appraisers and therefore tend to co-vary. Kitchen quality, overall quality, and exterior quality were among the most associated categorical pairs.

Numerical Quality Assessment

A structured quality report was produced for all numeric variables, computing mean-minus-median gap, coefficient of variation, skewness, and excess kurtosis. Variables with high absolute skewness (above 1.0) were flagged as candidates for log transformation. SalePrice itself has a skewness of approximately 1.76, confirming the need for transformation. Area and count variables also exhibit moderate to high right skew.

03

Data Preprocessing

All preprocessing decisions are conditioned on training-set statistics only. No quantity estimated from validation or test data is used anywhere in the pipeline.

Missing Value Treatment

Structural Variables

For the 25 structurally missing variables, missingness is filled with semantically appropriate constants. Categorical structural variables (basement quality, garage quality, pool quality, fence, alley type, fireplace quality, masonry veneer type, and miscellaneous features) are filled with the string 'None', which preserves the categorical meaning of absence as an explicit level. Numeric structural variables (masonry veneer area, all basement and garage area and count fields) are filled with zero, correctly encoding the absence of the corresponding physical feature.

Non-Structural Variables

Lot Frontage is imputed conditionally on neighborhood. For each neighborhood, the median frontage in the training set is computed and applied to missing observations in that neighborhood across all three splits. For neighborhoods that appear in validation or test but have no training observations with missing frontage (an edge case), the global training median serves as fallback. This neighborhood-stratified approach produces lower imputation bias than a global median because frontage values are spatially clustered.

Electrical has a single missing observation. Given the near-trivial scale of this gap, a global mode imputation on the training set is applied. The most common electrical system type — standard circuit breaker — is used to fill the missing entry.

Feature Engineering

Six derived features were constructed from domain knowledge:

  • Total_SF = Total Bsmt SF + Gr Liv Area. Aggregates basement and above-ground living area into a single total indoor area measure, reducing the multicollinearity among disaggregated area variables identified in EDA.
  • House_Age = Yr Sold − Year Built. Property age at the time of sale, capturing physical depreciation and obsolescence.
  • Years_Since_Remod = Yr Sold − Year Remod/Add. Renovation recency, which distinguishes between an old property that has been modernized and one that has not.
  • Total_Bath = Full Bath + 0.5 × Half Bath + Bsmt Full Bath + 0.5 × Bsmt Half Bath. A weighted count of all bathrooms, reflecting that half bathrooms add partial value.
  • Qual_Cond = Overall Qual × Overall Cond. A composite quality-condition score, tested as an alternative representation of overall property quality.
  • Has_Garage = 1 if Garage Area > 0, else 0. A binary availability indicator that separates structural absence from positive capacity.

Categorical Grouping

Three categorical variables were simplified into grouped versions fitted on the training set only.

Neighborhood grouping: Neighborhoods with a training frequency below 5% are consolidated into an 'Other' category, yielding Neighborhood_grouped. A further simplification, Neighborhood_simple, collapses all neighborhoods into two groups: High (NridgHt and Somerst, which exhibit consistently high sale prices) and Other.

Kitchen quality grouping: The five ordinal levels (Po, Fa, TA, Gd, Ex) are mapped to three: Low (Po, Fa), Medium (TA), and High (Gd, Ex). This reduces parameter count while preserving the ordinal gradient.

Building type simplification: The five building type levels are collapsed to Detached (single-family, 1Fam) and Other, reflecting the large price gap between detached and attached configurations.

Classification Target Construction

The binary target is_high was constructed by computing the 33rd and 66th percentiles of SalePrice on the training set and assigning each observation to Low, Medium, or High accordingly. is_high is 1 for the High tier and 0 otherwise. Approximately one third of training observations are in the High tier. The tier thresholds are fixed from training data and applied unchanged to validation and test.

04

Regression Models

Seven ordinary least squares models were estimated with log(SalePrice) as the target. Log transformation addresses right skew in the outcome and stabilises residual variance. Coefficients are interpretable as approximate percentage changes in sale price.

Model 1 — Baseline Reference

Specification: log(SalePrice) ~ Overall Qual + Total_SF + House_Age

Three predictors representing the dominant structural determinants of residential value: appraisal quality score, total floor area, and property age. No log transformations or categorical terms are included. The purpose is to establish a reference performance level.

The adjusted R² of approximately 0.808 indicates that these three variables alone explain over 80% of variance in log sale price — a remarkably strong baseline that reflects the dataset's well-structured nature. Each unit increase in Overall Qual is associated with approximately an 11–13% increase in sale price. House_Age carries a negative coefficient, consistent with depreciation. Despite this explanatory power, the original-scale RMSE is high, as log-scale residuals are amplified when exponentiated at the upper end of the price distribution.

Model 5 — Neighborhood × Size Interaction Rejected

Specification: Model 4 + C(Neighborhood_simple) : log(Total_SF)

This model tests a specific economic hypothesis: that the marginal price premium of additional living area differs between premium and standard neighborhoods. The interaction term between the binary neighborhood indicator and log area would, if significant, imply that a square foot is valued differently depending on location.

The interaction term introduces VIF values exceeding 1,000 for the interaction terms and their constituent main effects. At this scale, coefficient estimates are numerically unstable and standard errors are inflated to the point where no hypothesis test is valid. Validation RMSE does not improve relative to Model 4. Model 5 is rejected on both stability and performance grounds. The result illustrates a general principle: interaction terms between a continuous variable and a binary dummy derived from the same design matrix will almost always produce severe collinearity.

Model 7 — Qual_Cond Substitution Experiment Controlled test

Specification: Model 4, with Overall Qual replaced by Qual_Cond

A controlled substitution test that replaces the simple quality rating with the composite quality-condition product. All other elements of the Model 4 specification are unchanged.

Qual_Cond produces a marginal log-scale RMSE improvement over Model 4. However, when it replaces Overall Qual, the variable Years_Since_Remod loses statistical significance (p ≈ 0.095). The slight performance gain does not justify the loss of a significant predictor and the associated reduction in interpretability. This result justifies retaining Overall Qual in the preferred specification.

Note on model selection

Model 3 achieves the best validation RMSE but was not selected as the final model. Its full neighborhood encoding introduces many low-frequency dummies that increase the risk of distributional shift on unseen data. Model 4, with its simplified binary and three-level categorical groupings, sacrifices a modest amount of validation performance in exchange for substantially greater stability and interpretability — a trade-off that is appropriate when the model is intended to generalize beyond the training distribution.

Key Findings from Regression Sequence

  • Feature engineering and log transformations (Model 2) produced a larger RMSE reduction over the baseline than categorical predictors alone, confirming that how variables are represented matters as much as which variables are included.
  • Neighborhood is the most powerful categorical predictor. Its inclusion (Model 3) produces the single largest drop in RMSE across the entire sequence, confirming that location carries pricing information that no numeric variable can proxy.
  • Categorical simplification (Model 4) accepts a moderate performance cost in exchange for full coefficient significance and resistance to sparse-level overfitting. All twelve parameters are statistically significant.
  • The interaction model (Model 5) demonstrates that theoretical plausibility does not guarantee numerical viability. VIF values above 1,000 render the model's coefficients meaningless regardless of fit statistics.
  • The reduced model (Model 6) confirms that the additional predictors in Model 4 are all necessary: no single group can be dropped without meaningful performance loss.
  • The Qual_Cond substitution (Model 7) illustrates a collinearity mechanism: composite features that aggregate correlated dimensions can absorb variance from related variables and reduce the independence of other predictors in the model.

Classification Models

Five logistic regression models were estimated to classify properties into the High price tier. All models use a classification threshold of 0.4, selected to prioritise recall for the high-price class.

Threshold selection

The default logistic regression threshold of 0.5 was reduced to 0.4. In a real estate context, the cost of failing to identify an expensive property (false negative) is higher than the cost of incorrectly flagging a mid-price property (false positive). Reducing the threshold increases sensitivity for the High class at a modest precision cost — an asymmetric trade-off that is appropriate given the asymmetric cost structure of the task.

Logit 1 — Baseline Reference

Specification: is_high ~ Overall Qual + log(Total_SF)

Two predictors: overall quality and log of total area. An intentionally parsimonious starting point that tests whether the dominant drivers of sale price are sufficient for binary tier classification.

ROC-AUC of approximately 0.9607 on the validation set. This is a strong result for a two-variable model, confirming that quality and size jointly dominate the classification signal. The quality coefficient is substantially larger than the area coefficient, reflecting greater discriminatory power per unit of quality. The high baseline establishes that all subsequent models will face diminishing returns.

The test set was withheld throughout the model development process. It was used once, after all model selection decisions were finalised on the validation set, to obtain an unbiased estimate of generalization performance.

Model 4 was applied to the test set as the designated final regression model. It was selected over Model 3 — which produced lower validation RMSE — because its simplified categorical structure is more robust to distributional shift and its coefficients are fully interpretable.

09

Conclusions

The analysis demonstrates that residential sale price in Ames, Iowa is highly predictable from a compact set of structural, locational, and temporal property characteristics.

Regression

The regression baseline with three predictors explains over 80% of variance in log sale price, establishing that the dataset is well-structured for linear modeling. Systematic feature engineering and log transformation of skewed area variables produce the largest single improvement over the baseline, confirming that representational choices matter independently of variable selection. The addition of categorical predictors — particularly neighborhood — achieves the best validation RMSE in Model 3, demonstrating that location carries structural pricing information that cannot be proxied by numeric variables.

The preferred final model (Model 4) accepts a moderate performance cost relative to Model 3 in exchange for a fully interpretable, stable specification with twelve significant parameters and low multicollinearity. The test-set RMSE of 0.1546 (log scale) / $29,476 (original scale) is consistent with validation performance and provides no evidence of overfitting.

Two controlled experiments clarify the boundaries of the modeling approach. The interaction model (Model 5) demonstrates that neighborhood-by-size interactions, while theoretically appealing, introduce multicollinearity so severe that the model is numerically invalid. The Qual_Cond substitution (Model 7) shows that composite quality features can absorb variance from related predictors in ways that reduce interpretability — a phenomenon that motivates the retention of the simpler, cleaner Overall Qual specification.

Classification

The high-price classification task is tractable even at the baseline: quality and total area alone achieve a ROC-AUC of 0.96, confirming that the High price tier is predominantly determined by a small number of dominant attributes. The enriched Logit 3 model raises the AUC to 0.975 by adding lot area, bathroom count, and temporal variables. Subsequent parsimony reduction (Logit 4, Logit Final) preserves this performance level exactly, confirming that the peak discriminatory capacity of the feature set is reached at this complexity level.

The Logit Final model achieves a test ROC-AUC of 0.9825 — exceeding its validation performance — with accuracy of 0.93 and recall of 0.94 for high-price properties. These results confirm strong generalization: the model correctly identifies the vast majority of expensive properties on data it has never seen.

Key Drivers of Residential Value

  • Property size (Total_SF, Lot Area) is a primary driver in every model. The log-linear specification captures diminishing marginal returns.
  • Overall quality rating is the single strongest predictor across both modeling tasks, confirming that appraiser quality scores are highly informative signals of market value.
  • Neighborhood contributes pricing information that no numeric variable can proxy. The premium neighborhood effect adds approximately 10–15% to predicted sale price in the preferred regression model.
  • Bathroom count and garage area add independent value beyond total living area, with bathroom count proving more robust across the classification sequence.
  • Property age and renovation recency jointly capture temporal depreciation and modernization effects. Both variables are significant predictors; renovation recency is sensitive to the inclusion of composite quality features.
  • Kitchen quality adds marginal incremental value. It is significant in isolation but dispensable once the primary drivers are controlled for — a result with practical implications for data collection in deployment settings.
